There's a particular kind of calm that comes from a plain text file. No loading
spinner, no sync conflict, no subscription. Just bytes you can read in forty
years with a tool that hasn't been invented yet.

## It outlives its tools

Formats come and go. The `.txt` you wrote in 1995 still opens. That durability
isn't an accident — it's the *absence* of cleverness. Nothing to break because
there was never much there to begin with.

> The best file format is the one you never have to think about.

A few things I keep coming back to:

- **Greppable.** Your whole life, searchable with one command.
- **Diff-able.** Version control turns prose into something you can reason about.
- **Portable.** It moves between machines without a second thought.

## The catch

Plain text asks you to bring the structure yourself. That's the trade: total
freedom, zero scaffolding. For most of what I write, that's exactly the deal I
want.
